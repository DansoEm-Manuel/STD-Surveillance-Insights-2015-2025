# A Decade of STD Trends: From Data to Action (2015–2025)

## Project Overview

This capstone project was completed as part of the Blossom Academy SCILL Data Analytics Cohort.

The project analyzes global Sexually Transmitted Disease (STD) trends between 2015 and 2025 to identify disease burden patterns, mortality trends, regional disparities, and opportunities for targeted public health interventions.

Using data analytics and visualization techniques, the project transforms complex health data into actionable insights for policymakers, healthcare organizations, and global health stakeholders.

## Problem Statement

Despite advances in prevention, treatment, and awareness, sexually transmitted diseases continue to pose significant public health challenges worldwide. This project seeks to answer:

- Which STDs contribute most to the global disease burden?
- Which regions experience the highest incidence and mortality rates?
- What trends have emerged over the past decade?
- What interventions could help reduce future cases and deaths?

## Key Insights

- Over 336 million reported STD cases were analyzed.
- Africa experiences a disproportionate share of severe outcomes.
- HIV/AIDS remains a major contributor to prevalence and mortality.
- Mortality rates are strongly influenced by healthcare system gaps and late-stage diagnosis.
- Early detection, vaccination, and targeted interventions remain critical for reducing disease burden.

## Tools Used

- Power BI
- Microsoft Excel
- Data Cleaning & Transformation
- Data Visualization
- Statistical Analysis

## Recommendations

1. Scale early detection and screening programs.
2. Expand HIV/AIDS treatment access.
3. Increase Hepatitis B and HPV vaccination coverage.
4. Implement geographically targeted interventions.
5. Strengthen healthcare systems and long-term funding programs.

## Project Outcome

The analysis demonstrates that while significant progress has been made, sustained investment, targeted interventions, and stronger healthcare systems are required to reduce the global burden of STDs over the next decade.
